C1-to-C2 column bound: A written column is the value function after one mov or cmov; each write counts separately, including a copy. A lattice column equals a min/max expression in the inputs. The C1 construction in w12/lemma.md removes garbage reads without increasing core length, but it can change comparison outcomes and intermediate columns. Its cost bound does not bound the number of full non-lattice written columns in the result. Thus C1 does not establish that every strictly correct k<=31 program with a garbage-dependent comparison has a representative in C2 V2, which permits at most two such columns. To close this gap, prove that representative bound, provide checked exhaustive coverage of the additional resulting classes, or explicitly exclude those classes from the final claim. C2 V1 also requires lattice comparison operands; the bound alone would support V2 only.

Mixed three-column reduction: Let q count garbage-partial written columns and r count original full non-lattice written columns; every other written column must be lattice. With defined comparison operands, the proved reduction gives at most max(0,q-1) full non-lattice written columns only when r=0. The general garbage-removal construction preserves original full columns and bounds the resulting count by q+r. It does not establish a bound of two for (q,r)=(1,2) or (2,1), because the first partial write can copy an earlier non-lattice value. The case (q,r)=(0,3) already has three full non-lattice columns and has no partial write to remove. To close the gap for q+r<=3, prove a same-or-shorter reduction to at most two full non-lattice written columns for these three cases, provide checked exhaustive coverage of their remaining classes, or narrow the stated scope. All comparison operands must remain defined; C1 needs the separate bound above if it changes that hypothesis or the column count.
